# Your AI Tools Have a Spend Problem

Most startup teams now buy AI tools the way they used to buy coffee: one seat at a time, no procurement process, and no one really wants to be the person who says no. Cursor for engineers. ChatGPT for product. Claude for writing and code. Copilot for the GitHub-native folks. A direct OpenAI or Anthropic API bill that started as an experiment and quietly became infrastructure.

The problem is not that these tools are bad. The problem is that AI spend is new enough that most teams have no benchmark. A founder sees the bill, feels vaguely annoyed, and pays it because the team says the tools are useful.

StackLens is a free AI spend audit for that exact moment. Add your tools, plans, seats, monthly spend, team size, and primary use case. StackLens gives you an immediate report: where you are overspending, what to downgrade or switch, how your spend per teammate compares with similar teams, and whether discounted credits could reduce retail spend without disrupting workflows.

No login. No email gate before the result. If the report is useful, you can capture it, export a PDF, or share a public URL that strips personal details.

If your audit shows more than $500/month in savings, StackLens also shows where Credex can help. Credex sources discounted AI infrastructure credits from companies that overforecasted or pivoted, so some teams can keep using the same underlying AI products while paying less than retail.

Run the audit before your next AI invoice becomes "just how much this costs now."
