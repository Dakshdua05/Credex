# User Interviews

This file must contain notes from three real conversations. I am not fabricating these, because the assignment explicitly says fabricated interviews are an instant reject.

Use this exact structure after you complete each 10-15 minute interview.

- **Name or initials:** P.K.
- **Role:** College Student
- **Stage:** Final-year undergraduate student
- **Direct quote 1:** "I pay for ChatGPT Plus and Canva Pro for assignments and presentations."
- **Direct quote 2:** "I decided to buy them myself because they save me a lot of time."
- **Direct quote 3:** "I didn't realize how fast AI image credits run out during project season."
- **Most surprising thing they said:** The student was paying for two note-summarizing apps that basically did the same thing.
- **What it changed:** This reinforced that overlap detection should be visible in the audit. Even small buyers can end up paying twice for the same job when tools are purchased one assignment or workflow at a time.

P.K.'s response showed that AI spend habits form early, before someone is managing a company budget. The motivation was practical: tools saved time during assignments and presentations. The pain came from usage-based image credits and duplicate note apps, not from one expensive enterprise plan. This is a useful reminder for StackLens copy: users understand "same job, two tools" faster than they understand procurement language.

### Student 2 - S.R.

- **Name or initials:** S.R.
- **Role:** MBA Student
- **Stage:** Internship and placement preparation stage
- **Direct quote 1:** "I use ChatGPT, Grammarly, and Perplexity almost every day."
- **Direct quote 2:** "My friends influenced most of my AI purchases because everyone was talking about productivity."
- **Direct quote 3:** "The annual subscription looked cheap initially, but combined costs became stressful later."
- **Most surprising thing they said:** The student forgot about an auto-renewal and got charged for a full year unexpectedly.
- **What it changed:** This made renewal timing feel like a useful future feature. Even when a plan is not overpriced, the user may need reminders before annual renewals stack up.

S.R.'s notes pointed to social proof as a driver of AI purchases. Tools were bought because peers talked about productivity, and only later did the combined cost become stressful. The strongest product implication is that StackLens should not only recommend immediate cuts; it could eventually track renewal dates and warn users before annual plans renew. For the current assignment, it supports the "notify me when new optimizations apply" lead capture path.

### Student 3 - A.M.

- **Name or initials:** A.M.
- **Role:** Design Student and Freelancer
- **Stage:** Freelancing while studying
- **Direct quote 1:** "I pay for Midjourney, ChatGPT Plus, and an AI video editing app."
- **Direct quote 2:** "I bought them because clients expect faster delivery now."
- **Direct quote 3:** "I trust savings recommendations only if they clearly compare features I actually use."
- **Most surprising thing they said:** The AI video tool charged extra for exporting higher-quality videos, which was not obvious initially.
- **What it changed:** This reinforced that recommendations need reasons tied to use case, not just cheaper prices. A freelancer will not downgrade if the cheaper plan breaks client delivery.

A.M.'s response was the closest student response to a business buyer because the tools were tied to client delivery. The hidden export charge was a reminder that public plan prices often miss the actual monthly bill. Their trust requirement also matched the operations interview: people need feature-level reasoning before accepting a savings recommendation. This supports the audit design choice to show one-sentence reasons next to every recommendation.

### Common Shareability Insight

- P.K.: "Yes, because students need affordable options."
- S.R.: "Definitely, especially for comparing useful subscriptions."
- A.M.: "Yes, if personal payment details are hidden."

All three student respondents said they would share an anonymized savings report if personal payment details were hidden. That supports the public report design, especially the decision to strip email, company, and role from share URLs.
