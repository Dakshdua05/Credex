# Economics

Assumptions: Credex sells discounted AI infrastructure credits and earns a margin on credit resale or procurement facilitation. A realistic early converted lead might be a startup spending $2,000/month on AI tools and API usage. If Credex helps shift $1,000/month of that spend into discounted credits and keeps a 12% gross margin, annual gross profit is $1,440. If the average successful customer expands to $2,500/month of routed credits, annual gross profit becomes $3,600. I would use $2,000 gross profit per converted customer as a conservative lead value for planning.

Channel CAC from the GTM plan is mostly time cost. If founder/operator time is valued at $50/hour, a Hacker News launch that takes 6 hours and produces 100 audits has a $3 audit CAC. If direct DMs take 10 hours and produce 20 audits, CAC is $25 per audit. Reddit/community answering might take 8 hours for 40 audits, or $10 per audit. The blended unpaid CAC target should be under $15 per completed audit.

Profitability math: with a $2,000 gross-profit customer value and $15 completed-audit CAC, the funnel can be very inefficient and still work. If 100 audits cost $1,500 equivalent effort, 25 become leads, 5 book consultations, and 1 buys credits, Credex gets about $2,000 gross profit and a positive first-order payback. That implies 25% audit-to-lead, 20% lead-to-consult, and 20% consult-to-purchase, or 1% audit-to-purchase overall.

For $1M ARR in 18 months, Credex needs either roughly $83,333/month of revenue from this channel or a meaningful gross-profit equivalent. If average routed credit volume is $2,500/month per customer and Credex's take rate is 12%, revenue is $300/customer/month. That requires about 278 active customers. At 1% audit-to-purchase, the tool needs about 27,800 completed audits over 18 months, or 1,545 per month. If conversion improves to 3% for high-savings audits, the requirement drops below 10,000 audits total.

The biggest economic risk is low-intent audits from curious users with no meaningful spend. The product should qualify on monthly savings and route low-savings users into nurture, not sales calls.
